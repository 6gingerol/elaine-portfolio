From requirements to flows

I started by mapping the main journey, then kept moving between sketches, layout decisions, and small interaction tests. The point was to keep the playful parts, but not let them get in the way of the structure underneath.

The biggest shift came from realizing the experience needed clearer signposting. That pushed the design toward stronger hierarchy and more deliberate transitions.

One decision at a time

The hardest call was how much information to show at once. A denser layout could have exposed more upfront, but it made the experience harder to scan. Instead, I broke the flow into clearer steps and let each screen carry one job.

That kept the system feeling simpler and made each state easier to understand, which mattered more than trying to show everything at once.

The shipped experience

The result is a JellyBreak flow that feels more intentional: the core interaction is easier to follow, the hierarchy is clearer, and the playful parts still come through.
